How to become a minister

Become an ordained minister online, free, in about five minutes. Then officiate weddings, lead funerals, perform baptisms, or start your own ministry.

By the ULC Monastery Ministry TeamReviewed Oct 28, 20269 min read
Quick answerTo become a minister, fill out the free ordination form with the Universal Life Church Monastery. You're ordained the moment you finish. If you'll officiate a wedding, check your state's rules and order credentials if the county asks.

Become an ordained minister in 3 steps

  1. Fill out the free form. Your name and email, then your state and a password. Start now →
  2. Check your state's rules. Most states don't require officiant registration; a few do. Our marriage law guides explain each one.
  3. Get credentials if needed. A credential or letter of good standing is proof of ordination.

What can an ordained minister do?

Officiate weddings

Legally solemnize marriages in nearly every state.

Lead funerals and memorials

Guide a family through a service that honors a life.

Perform baptisms and blessings

Welcome new life, or bless a home, a pet, a new start.

Start your own ministry

Gather a congregation and practice what you believe.

How to officiate a funeral

Many new ministers are first asked to be a funeral officiant. Funerals aren't licensed like weddings, so the family can choose anyone, and an ordained minister brings structure and calm. Meet the family first, gather stories, plan the order of service (welcome, readings, eulogy, prayer or reflection, committal), and keep it to 20–40 minutes.

How much does it cost to become a minister?

Nothing. ULC ordination is free and never expires. Credentials ($14.99 and up) and clergy apparel are optional.

FAQ

How long does it take to become a minister?
About five minutes. Your ordination is effective immediately.
Do I need to belong to a particular faith?
No. The Universal Life Church welcomes every faith, and none.
Is becoming a minister online legal?
Yes. ULC ordinations are recognized in nearly every U.S. state. See the legal record.
