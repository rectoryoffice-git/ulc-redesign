Everything you need to know — the steps, the cost, the timing, and whether it is legal where you live.

The short answer

Fill out the free application on ULC.org — it takes a few minutes — and you're ordained immediately. Ordination is permanent, costs nothing, and is recognized for officiating weddings across the U.S. Some counties ask to see printed proof, and a few states require officiants to register first.

What does it mean to be ordained?

Ordination confers the legal title of minister, with the same rights to solemnize marriages as clergy of other faiths. ULC ministers lead weddings, baptisms, funerals, and blessings, and some go on to build their own ministries.

The Universal Life Church asks its ministers to uphold two tenets only:

  1. Do that which is right.
  2. Practice your religion however you see fit, so long as you abide by the law and respect the rights of others.

How long does it take?

The application takes a few minutes. You're ordained as soon as you submit it. If you need printed documents, standard shipping usually arrives in 3–10 business days; the Emergency Wedding Set ships FedEx Overnight.

Does ordination expire?

No. Your ordination is permanent. You never need to renew or pay to stay in good standing.

Is online ordination legal in my state?

Yes. The ULC has active ministers in every state. Requirements differ: most states need nothing beyond ordination, while some ask officiants to register (for example Nevada, Hawaii, and New York City). Choose a state for its exact rules:
