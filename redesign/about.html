Legally backed

Your ordination stands on solid ground

ULC ministers officiate legal weddings across the country, and the ULC defends its ministers' rights in court.

Nationwide

Recognized in nearly every state

ULC ministers can legally officiate weddings in nearly every U.S. state. A few, such as Virginia, have extra rules, and our state guides spell them out.

Federal court · 2023

Defended in Tennessee

After the ULC sued over a 2019 Tennessee law, the district attorneys it sued agreed in federal court not to challenge marriages performed by ULC ministers.

ULC Monastery Storehouse v. Nabors, M.D. Tenn.
Federal court · 2002

Online ordination upheld

A federal court struck down Utah's ban on wedding officiants ordained online, and permanently barred the state from enforcing it.

Universal Life Church v. Utah, D. Utah

This is general information, not legal advice. Marriage rules vary by state and county, so always check with the clerk who issues the license.

What we believe

Two tenets. No hierarchy. No tests.

I

Do that which is right.

II

Practice your religion as you see fit, so long as you respect the rights and freedoms of others and abide by the law.

Our mission

Spiritual duties are a right, not a privilege.

For centuries, ordination meant years of study, expensive seminary and, for many, being the "wrong" gender or belief. The ULC removed those barriers. Whether you worship one God, many, or none — whether you officiate one ceremony or a hundred — we stand with you.

ULC ministers were also part of the movement for marriage equality and officiated some of the first same-sex weddings after that victory.
