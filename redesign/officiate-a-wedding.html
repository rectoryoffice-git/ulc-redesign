How to officiate a wedding

Four steps, start to finish — and the exact rules for the state where the wedding is happening.

Not ordained yet? Get ordained free first →

Quick answer

To officiate a wedding: (1) get ordained, free and online with the ULC; (2) check the state and county rules, and register if required; (3) plan a ceremony that includes the couple's declaration of intent and your pronouncement; (4) sign the marriage license and return it before the deadline.

Officiant FAQ

Common questions

Can a friend or family member legally officiate?
Yes. Once ordained, a friend or relative has the same authority to solemnize a marriage as any other minister.
What has to be said for the marriage to be legal?
At minimum, a declaration of intent — the couple's "I do" — and your pronouncement that they are married. Everything else is up to you and the couple.
Do I need witnesses?
It depends on the state. Most require one or two adult witnesses; some, like Texas, require none. Your state guide lists the number and minimum age.
Who returns the marriage license?
Usually the officiant. Return deadlines range from a few days to 30 days or more — missing one can delay the marriage being recorded.
