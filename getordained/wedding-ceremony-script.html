Free wedding ceremony script

A complete wedding officiant script you can read word for word, a non-religious version, and tips for the officiant speech.

By the Get Ordained Wedding TeamReviewed Oct 28, 2026
Quick answerEvery ceremony needs the same core: a welcome, the couple's consent ("I do"), the vows and rings, and your pronouncement. Everything else (readings, stories, rituals) is up to the couple.

Wedding officiant script: traditional ceremony

Processional. Guests rise as the wedding party enters.

Welcome. "Friends and family, welcome. We are gathered here today to celebrate the love between [Partner A] and [Partner B], and to witness them joined in marriage."

Reading. A friend reads a passage the couple chose.

Officiant's words. A short reflection on the couple: how they met, what makes them them.

Declaration of consent. "[Partner A], do you take [Partner B] to be your lawfully wedded spouse, to have and to hold, from this day forward?" — "I do." (Repeat for Partner B.)

Vows. "I, [Partner A], take you, [Partner B], to be my spouse, to love and to cherish, for better, for worse, in sickness and in health."

Ring exchange. "With this ring, I thee wed."

Pronouncement. "By the power vested in me by the Universal Life Church and the State of [State], I now pronounce you married. You may kiss!"

Presentation. "It is my honor to present, for the first time, the newlyweds!"

Wedding ceremony script: non-religious version

Many couples want a ceremony without religious language. Keep the legal core and swap the rest:

Welcome. "Thank you all for being here. [Partner A] and [Partner B] asked the people who matter most to them to witness this promise."

Consent. "[Partner A], do you choose [Partner B], today and every day?" — "I do."

Pronouncement. "By the authority granted to me by the State of [State], I now pronounce you married."

Wedding officiant speech: tips

  • Keep it to 2–4 minutes. The couple is the story, not you.
  • Tell one specific story that shows who they are together.
  • Practice out loud at the rehearsal, and print your script in a large font.
  • Say the legal parts clearly: consent and the pronouncement.

After the ceremony

Sign the marriage license with the couple and return it on time. Check the marriage laws for your state for the deadline.
