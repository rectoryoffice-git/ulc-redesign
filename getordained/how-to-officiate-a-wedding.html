How to officiate a wedding

Asked to officiate? Here's how to become a wedding officiant and lead a ceremony your friends will remember, from ordination to signing the license.

By the Get Ordained Wedding TeamReviewed Oct 28, 202612 min read
Quick answerTo officiate a wedding, get ordained online (free, about 5 minutes), check the marriage laws for the state where the wedding is, get proof of ordination if the county asks, write the ceremony, lead it, and sign and return the marriage license on time.

1. How to become a wedding officiant

Anyone 18 or older can get ordained online with the Universal Life Church. It's free, it takes about five minutes, and your ordination never expires. You don't need a seminary degree or to belong to a particular faith.

2. Check the state's marriage laws

Officiant rules are set by the state where the wedding takes place, not where you live. Most states have no officiant registration; a few, and some counties, ask for proof of ordination or registration before the ceremony.

What to checkOfficiant registration, proof of ordination, witnesses, license deadlines
Who to askThe county clerk issuing the marriage license
WhenAt least a month before the wedding

3. Get proof of ordination (if asked)

Your ordination is valid as soon as you finish the application. If the county, venue or couple wants paperwork, the Classic Wedding Kit covers most counties. If the wedding is under 10 days away, choose the Emergency Wedding Kit, which ships overnight with a notarized Letter of Good Standing.

4. Plan the ceremony with the couple

  • Ask what they want the ceremony to feel like: religious, spiritual, or not religious at all.
  • Agree on length (most ceremonies run 15–30 minutes), readings, and whether they'll write their own vows.
  • Confirm who brings the license, and who signs as witnesses if the state requires them.
  • Run through it at the rehearsal.

5. Write the ceremony: a simple wedding officiant script

Use this short wedding ceremony script as a starting point, or see our full free wedding ceremony script. The legal heart of any ceremony is short: the couple's consent and your pronouncement.

Welcome. "Friends and family, we are gathered here today to celebrate the love of [Partner A] and [Partner B], and to witness them joined in marriage."

A few words. Share a short story about the couple, or a reading they love.

Consent. "[Partner A], do you take [Partner B] to be your spouse, to love and support, from this day forward?" — "I do." (Repeat for Partner B.)

Vows and rings. "With this ring, I give you my promise…"

Pronouncement. "By the power vested in me by the Universal Life Church and the State of [State], I now pronounce you married. You may kiss!"

Want it personalized? Our script generator builds a full ceremony from a few questions.

6. How to perform a wedding ceremony

Speak slowly, look up from your notes, and enjoy it. Hear the couple's consent and say the pronouncement clearly. Those are the parts the law cares about.

7. Sign and return the marriage license

Fill in your title (Minister), the ordaining church (Universal Life Church), and the date and place of the ceremony. Return the license to the issuing office before the deadline; in Texas, for example, that's within 30 days.

FAQ

Can a friend officiate a wedding?
Yes. In nearly every U.S. state, a friend or family member who has been ordained can legally officiate.
Do I need to register before the wedding?
Only in a few states and counties. Your state guide will tell you.
How much does it cost to become a wedding officiant?
Ordination is free. Optional documents start at $11.99.
